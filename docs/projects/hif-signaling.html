Mechanistic Modeling of HIF Signaling

Resolving the HIF1-to-HIF2 switch in endothelial cells under hypoxia

Mechanistic Systems Biology
HIF
Hypoxia
ODE Modeling
A validated mechanistic model of endothelial oxygen sensing and HIF isoform-specific regulation.
Published

June 19, 2026

Project overview

Hypoxia activates cellular oxygen-sensing mechanisms that promote adaptation, survival, and angiogenesis. Although HIF1 and HIF2 are both central to this response, they exhibit different temporal behaviors and regulatory roles that are difficult to resolve experimentally.

This project developed a mechanistic model of the PHD-HIF-VEGFA signaling axis in endothelial cells, with explicit representation of HIF1α, HIF2α, PHD2, PHD3, and VEGFA mRNA.

At a glance

  • Application: endothelial hypoxia signaling
  • Model type: mechanistic ordinary differential equation model
  • Calibration: particle swarm optimization followed by local refinement
  • Evaluation: identifiability analysis, uncertainty quantification, residual analysis, and independent validation
  • Software: MATLAB and SimBiology
  • Publication: iScience, 2026

Scientific question

How do oxygen availability, HIF isoform dynamics, and PHD-mediated feedback interact to regulate endothelial responses during acute and prolonged hypoxia?

The model was designed to investigate:

  • graded sensitivity to oxygen availability;
  • the transition from an acute HIF1-dominated response to a prolonged HIF2-dominated response;
  • the relative contributions of HIF1 and HIF2 to VEGFA regulation;
  • the distinct effects of PHD2 and PHD3 inhibition.

Modeling approach

The model integrates oxygen-dependent hydroxylation and degradation of HIF proteins, nuclear signaling, transcriptional feedback, and VEGFA mRNA regulation.

The development workflow included:

  1. literature-based model construction;
  2. structural identifiability analysis;
  3. practical identifiability analysis;
  4. global parameter estimation using particle swarm optimization;
  5. local parameter refinement;
  6. uncertainty quantification;
  7. validation against independent experimental datasets;
  8. predictive simulations under new oxygen and inhibition scenarios.

Key findings

Time-dependent HIF isoform switch

The model reproduced a rapid HIF1α response after the onset of hypoxia, followed by a delayed and more sustained HIF2α response. This captured the experimentally reported transition from acute HIF1-dominated signaling to prolonged HIF2-dominated signaling.

Oxygen-dependent response

Simulations predicted graded stabilization of HIF1α and HIF2α across oxygen levels, accompanied by dose- and time-dependent induction of VEGFA mRNA.

Distinct roles of PHD2 and PHD3

Perturbation simulations predicted that PHD2 and PHD3 have complementary, non-redundant, and isoform-specific effects on HIF stabilization and VEGFA regulation.

Fractional regulation of VEGFA

The model quantified the changing contributions of HIF1 and HIF2 to VEGFA mRNA regulation during prolonged hypoxia.

Model evaluation

The model was calibrated using endothelial-cell experimental data and validated against independent datasets not used for parameter estimation.

Across validation observables:

  • Pearson correlations ranged from 0.70 to 0.95;
  • Runs tests showed no systematic residual bias (p ≥ 0.35);
  • the model reproduced the major temporal trends of HIF1α, HIF2α, and VEGFA mRNA.

The analysis also documented limitations arising from heterogeneous experimental datasets, measurement platforms, and oxygen conditions.

My contribution

I co-developed the study concept, constructed the complete computational model, performed all simulations and quantitative analyses, and wrote the initial manuscript draft.

Scientific value

The model provides a technically evaluated and reusable representation of endothelial hypoxia signaling. It can serve as a module for larger models of ischemic disease, angiogenesis, tumor hypoxia, and quantitative systems pharmacology.
